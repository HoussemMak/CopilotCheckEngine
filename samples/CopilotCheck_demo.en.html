Why and how to configure
WhyRestricted Content Discovery is the interim measure Microsoft now points to as the reference way of removing a site from Copilot answers and organisation-wide search while permissions are being reviewed. It is a per-site setting that changes no permission: the content stays accessible to those entitled to it, but it disappears from Copilot grounding and the site loses its AI entry points (Copilot button, AI actions, agent creation, create pages with AI). Without that cover, an HR, Finance or Executive site identified as overshared stays queryable in natural language by the whole tenant for the weeks or months it takes to fix the access control lists, which is exactly the window where the exposure is known and untreated. This requirement is the counterpart of requirement 21, which inventories restricted sites: it asks the opposite question, whether the high-risk sites reported by the data access governance reports are actually covered. The tenant allow-list mechanism (Restricted SharePoint Search, requirement 22) is not a substitute: it is capped at 100 sites, degrades search for every user and is being retired. Use must remain selective: applied broadly, the restriction removes legitimate content from Copilot and degrades answer relevance across the tenant. Finally, latency matters: on a site holding more than 500,000 items, indexing can take more than a week, which has to be anticipated before the go-live date.

Why and how to configure
WhyAgents created in SharePoint are .agent files stored in each site's assets library: their governance goes through neither the Microsoft 365 app catalogue, nor Copilot Studio, nor the agent settings of the admin centre. They therefore fall entirely outside the requirements of this baseline that cover agents and extensions, which makes them a complete blind spot. A user who creates an agent on an overshared site produces a conversational entry point into all of that site's content, available to anyone who can reach the site, and blocking an agent from the Microsoft 365 admin centre currently applies to Copilot Chat only, neither to SharePoint nor to OneDrive. Microsoft now provides a dedicated report through SharePoint Online PowerShell, with three contents: agents per site, top 100 sites, and distribution. It makes sprawl measurable and lets the sites concerned be cross-checked against the list of sensitive or overshared sites. Without that inventory, the organisation does not know how many agents exist, on which sites they were created, or whether one of them opens a conversation over a site the permission review has just flagged. The report is triggered and read later: the audit engine only reads pre-existing reports and triggers a generation solely on explicit operator request, so as to remain free of side effects.
